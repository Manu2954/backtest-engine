================================================================================
                    WHAT IS AN ORDER BLOCK? (DEFINITION)
================================================================================

Conceptual Definition:
----------------------
An Order Block (OB) is a consolidation area or specific candle where institutional
traders (banks, hedge funds, market makers) placed significant orders that moved
the market. When price returns to this zone, the remaining unfilled orders can
act as support (bullish OB) or resistance (bearish OB).

Why It Works (Theory):
----------------------
1. Institutions can't fill large orders at once without moving price
2. They use limit orders spread across a price range
3. Not all orders get filled during initial move
4. When price revisits, remaining orders provide liquidity
5. Creates support/resistance zones that are "real" (backed by actual orders)

Market Structure Context:
-------------------------
Order Blocks typically form at:
- Swing lows (bullish OB) before uptrends
- Swing highs (bearish OB) before downtrends
- Areas where price reversed strongly (displacement)
- After liquidity grabs (stop hunts)


================================================================================
                    THE CHALLENGE: NO "OFFICIAL" DEFINITION
================================================================================

Problem:
--------
Unlike RSI or MACD, there is NO standardized mathematical formula for Order Blocks.
Different traders identify them using different rules, making algorithmic
detection subjective.

Why No Consensus:
-----------------
1. **Proprietary Origins**: ICT (Michael Huddleston) teaches OBs but hasn't
   published formal algorithms

2. **Visual Identification**: Most traders identify OBs manually by looking at
   charts, not following strict rules

3. **Context-Dependent**: What qualifies as an OB varies by:
   - Timeframe (5m vs 1h vs daily)
   - Asset (crypto vs stocks vs forex)
   - Market phase (trending vs ranging)
   - Trader's interpretation

4. **Hindsight Bias**: Easy to identify OBs in hindsight, harder in real-time

Result:
-------
Any algorithmic approach will be an APPROXIMATION of what manual traders identify.
Different algorithms will find different OBs. That's okay - we need backtestable
rules, even if imperfect.


================================================================================
                    COMMON ORDER BLOCK IDENTIFICATION RULES
================================================================================

These are the most widely accepted rules from SMC/ICT community:

BULLISH ORDER BLOCK:
--------------------
Identifies demand zones where institutions bought, creating upward pressure.

Rule Set A (Simplest):
  1. Find a DOWN candle (close < open) - "bearish candle"
  2. Check if followed by strong UPWARD move (displacement)
  3. Displacement = price moves up sharply (e.g., +2-5% in 1-3 candles)
  4. The DOWN candle before the move is the bullish OB
  5. OB zone = high to low of that candle

Rule Set B (With Structure):
  1. Identify swing low (local bottom in price)
  2. Find last DOWN candle before price broke above swing low
  3. Price must then make higher high (break structure)
  4. That last DOWN candle is bullish OB
  5. OB valid until price breaks below its low

Rule Set C (ICT-Style):
  1. Look for liquidity grab below swing low (stop hunt)
  2. Find strong reversal up (displacement candle)
  3. Last DOWN candle before reversal = OB
  4. Bonus: Check for imbalance/gap filled
  5. OB remains valid until "mitigated" (price trades through it)


BEARISH ORDER BLOCK:
--------------------
Identifies supply zones where institutions sold, creating downward pressure.

(Mirror opposite of bullish OB rules)

Rule Set A:
  1. Find an UP candle (close > open)
  2. Followed by strong DOWNWARD move
  3. The UP candle before the drop is bearish OB
  4. OB zone = low to high of that candle

Rule Set B:
  1. Identify swing high
  2. Find last UP candle before price broke below swing high
  3. Price must then make lower low
  4. That last UP candle is bearish OB

Rule Set C:
  1. Liquidity grab above swing high
  2. Strong reversal down
  3. Last UP candle before drop = OB


Key Variables to Define:
------------------------
1. **Displacement Threshold**: How much must price move? (2%? 5%? 10 ATRs?)
2. **Lookback Period**: How many candles to look back for OBs? (20? 50? 100?)
3. **Candle Color Requirement**: Must OB be opposite color? (strict vs flexible)
4. **Mitigation Rule**: When does OB become invalid? (price through midpoint? full body?)
5. **Strength Filter**: Only count OBs with X volume or Y% body size?


================================================================================
                    ALGORITHMIC APPROACHES (RANKED BY COMPLEXITY)
================================================================================

APPROACH 1: DISPLACEMENT-BASED (SIMPLEST)
------------------------------------------
Complexity: LOW
Accuracy: MODERATE (60-70% match with manual identification)

Logic:
  - Scan for strong directional moves (displacement)
  - Find the opposite-color candle immediately before move
  - Mark that candle as OB

Pseudocode:
  For each candle i:
    - Check if next 3 candles move strongly in one direction
    - If yes, check if candle[i] is opposite color
    - If yes, mark candle[i] as OB

Pros:
  ✅ Simple to implement
  ✅ Fast computation
  ✅ Works on any timeframe

Cons:
  ❌ Misses OBs without strong displacement
  ❌ No structure context (swing highs/lows)
  ❌ May mark too many OBs (false positives)

Parameters to Tune:
  - displacement_threshold: 2% default, adjust per asset volatility
  - displacement_candles: 1-3 candles for move
  - require_opposite_color: true/false


APPROACH 2: STRUCTURE-BASED (MEDIUM)
-------------------------------------
Complexity: MEDIUM
Accuracy: HIGH (70-85% match)

Logic:
  - First, identify swing highs and swing lows
  - Then find Break of Structure (BOS) moments
  - Look back from BOS to find last opposite-color candle
  - That's your OB

Pseudocode:
  Step 1: Find swing lows (local bottoms)
  Step 2: Detect when price breaks above swing low (BOS)
  Step 3: Look back N candles from BOS
  Step 4: Find last bearish candle before BOS
  Step 5: Mark as bullish OB

Pros:
  ✅ Aligns with market structure (more accurate)
  ✅ Fewer false positives
  ✅ Captures institutional behavior better

Cons:
  ❌ Swing detection itself is subjective
  ❌ More complex logic
  ❌ Slower computation (needs structure analysis first)

Parameters to Tune:
  - swing_detection_period: 10-50 candles
  - swing_threshold: X% or Y ATRs for swing validation
  - lookback_candles: How far back to search for OB (5-20)


APPROACH 3: LIQUIDITY + MITIGATION (COMPLEX)
---------------------------------------------
Complexity: HIGH
Accuracy: VERY HIGH (80-90% match)

Logic:
  - Detect liquidity pools (stop loss clusters)
  - Identify liquidity grabs (price sweeps then reverses)
  - Find OB that caused the reversal
  - Track OB mitigation (when price invalidates OB)

Pseudocode:
  Step 1: Mark liquidity zones (above swing highs, below swing lows)
  Step 2: Detect when price sweeps liquidity then reverses
  Step 3: Find the candle that initiated reversal
  Step 4: Mark as OB with mitigation tracking
  Step 5: OB remains "active" until price fully trades through it

Pros:
  ✅ Most accurate (closest to ICT methodology)
  ✅ Tracks OB lifecycle (active → mitigated)
  ✅ Filters for high-quality OBs only

Cons:
  ❌ Very complex to implement
  ❌ Computationally expensive
  ❌ Many subjective parameters

Parameters to Tune:
  - liquidity_detection: swing high/low ± X pips
  - reversal_threshold: Y% move after liquidity grab
  - mitigation_rule: 50% body through OB? Full body?
  - ob_strength_filter: Volume, body size, etc.

                    RECOMMENDED APPROACH FOR YOUR ENGINE
================================================================================

Given your goals (backtesting, SMC strategies, crypto/stocks), here's my recommendation:

PHASE 1: Start with DISPLACEMENT-BASED (2-3 weeks)
---------------------------------------------------
Why:
  - Fastest to implement and test
  - Establishes baseline: "Does OB concept add value at all?"
  - Can iterate quickly

Implementation:
  - Scan for 2-5% moves within 1-3 candles
  - Mark opposite-color candle before move
  - Store OB high/low as columns in DataFrame
  - Conditions reference: "is_in_bullish_ob"

Test:
  - Backtest simple strategy: "Enter when price touches bullish OB + RSI < 30"
  - Compare to: "Enter when RSI < 30" (no OB filter)
  - Does OB filter improve win rate? Sharpe ratio?

Success Metric:
  ✅ If OB filter improves results by 10%+ → Continue to Phase 2
  ❌ If no improvement → OB may not matter for your assets/timeframes


PHASE 2: Add STRUCTURE-BASED (2-3 weeks)
-----------------------------------------
Why:
  - More accurate OB detection
  - Aligns with true SMC methodology
  - Reduces false positives

Implementation:
  - First, build swing high/low detection
  - Then add Break of Structure (BOS) detection
  - Refine OB detection to use structure context

Test:
  - Compare displacement-only vs structure-based OBs
  - Which produces better backtest results?
  - Are there fewer but higher-quality OBs?

Success Metric:
  ✅ If structure improves results another 10%+ → Continue to Phase 3
  ❌ If no improvement → Stop here, displacement was good enough


PHASE 3: Add LIQUIDITY + MITIGATION (2-3 weeks) - OPTIONAL
-----------------------------------------------------------
Why:
  - Maximum accuracy
  - Closest to ICT methodology
  - Best for advanced traders

Implementation:
  - Liquidity pool detection (stop clusters)
  - Liquidity grab identification (sweep + reversal)
  - OB mitigation tracking (when OB becomes invalid)

Test:
  - Full SMC strategy: "Enter on liquidity grab → OB retest → displacement"
  - Compare to Phase 2 results

Success Metric:
  ✅ If Phase 3 improves another 10%+ → Keep it
  ❌ If marginal gains → Phase 2 is sufficient


ITERATIVE VALIDATION:
---------------------
After each phase:
  1. Backtest on 1 year of BTC, ETH, SPY data
  2. Compare metrics: Sharpe, win rate, max drawdown
  3. Manually inspect 20 identified OBs: "Do these look right?"
  4. Decide: Continue to next phase or stop here?


================================================================================
                    VALIDATION METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

How to know if your OB detection is "good enough":

QUANTITATIVE TESTS:
-------------------
1. **Backtest Performance**:
   - Strategy WITH OB filter vs WITHOUT
   - Does OB improve Sharpe by 0.2+?
   - Does OB improve win rate by 5%+?

2. **OB Touch Success Rate**:
   - When price touches OB, does it bounce? (measure %)
   - Expectation: 60-70% success rate for good OBs
   - Below 55%: Detection needs refinement

3. **False Positive Rate**:
   - How many detected OBs are never revisited?
   - Ideal: <30% of OBs ignored by price
   - High rate: Too many OBs being marked

4. **Comparison to Benchmarks**:
   - Support/Resistance levels (S/R)
   - Moving averages (e.g., SMA 200)
   - Pivot points
   - Are OBs better than these traditional levels?


QUALITATIVE TESTS:
------------------
1. **Manual Chart Review**:
   - Look at 50 random OBs detected by algorithm
   - Do they "make sense" visually?
   - Would a manual trader mark the same OB?
   - Aim for 70%+ agreement

2. **Edge Case Testing**:
   - Low volatility periods: Are OBs still valid?
   - High volatility: Too many OBs detected?
   - Trending vs ranging markets: Equal performance?

3. **Timeframe Consistency**:
   - Test on 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, 1d
   - OB concept should work across timeframes
   - Parameters may need adjustment per TF


CALIBRATION DATA:
-----------------
Use separate datasets for:
  - Training: Tune parameters (displacement %, lookback period)
  - Validation: Check if settings generalize
  - Test: Final performance measurement

Never optimize on the same data you backtest on!


================================================================================
                    KNOWN ISSUES & LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

Issue 1: OVERFITTING RISK
--------------------------
Problem: Easy to tune OB parameters until backtest looks perfect
Reality: Forward performance may disappoint
Solution: Use walk-forward testing, validate on unseen data


Issue 2: CONFIRMATION BIAS
---------------------------
Problem: You'll see OBs "work" because you expect them to
Reality: May be coincidence or self-fulfilling prophecy
Solution: Compare to random zones, benchmark against S/R levels


Issue 3: TIMEFRAME DEPENDENCE
------------------------------
Problem: OB on 5m chart may be noise, on 1d may be accurate
Reality: Higher timeframes likely more reliable
Solution: Test multi-timeframe OBs, weight by timeframe importance


Issue 4: MARKET REGIME CHANGES
-------------------------------
Problem: OBs work in ranging/choppy markets, less so in strong trends
Reality: Need to detect market regime first
Solution: Combine OB with trend filters (ADX, moving averages)


Issue 5: MITIGATION AMBIGUITY
------------------------------
Problem: When is OB "used up" and no longer valid?
Reality: Different traders have different rules
Solution: Test multiple mitigation rules, pick best via backtest


Issue 6: EXECUTION SLIPPAGE
----------------------------
Problem: Algo detects OB, but by time you enter, price already moved
Reality: Real-time detection has latency
Solution: Backtest with realistic fill assumptions (enter at next candle)


================================================================================
                    ALTERNATIVE: MACHINE LEARNING APPROACH
================================================================================

Instead of rule-based OB detection, could use ML:

Approach:
---------
1. Manually label 1000+ OBs on historical charts (ground truth)
2. Extract features: candle patterns, volume, structure, momentum
3. Train classifier: "Is this candle an Order Block? Yes/No"
4. Model learns patterns from your manual labels

Pros:
  ✅ Learns YOUR definition of OB (not someone else's)
  ✅ Can capture complex patterns rules miss
  ✅ Adapts to different markets/timeframes

Cons:
  ❌ Requires HUGE manual labeling effort (100+ hours)
  ❌ Black box: Can't explain why OB was identified
  ❌ May overfit to training data
  ❌ Complex to implement and maintain

Verdict:
--------
Consider ML only AFTER rule-based approach proves promising.
Don't start with ML - too much overhead for uncertain payoff.


================================================================================
                    RECOMMENDED PARAMETERS (STARTING POINT)
================================================================================

Based on community consensus and common implementations:

BULLISH ORDER BLOCK:
--------------------
displacement_threshold: 2.5% (adjust for volatility)
  - Low volatility assets (e.g., SPY): 1.5-2%
  - High volatility (e.g., BTC): 3-5%

displacement_candles: 1-3 candles
  - Shorter timeframes (5m, 15m): 1-2 candles
  - Longer timeframes (4h, 1d): 2-3 candles

require_opposite_color: True (strict) or False (flexible)
  - Start with True, relax to False if too few OBs

lookback_period: 20-50 candles
  - How far back to search for OBs
  - Longer = more OBs but older (less relevant)

ob_body_min: 30% of candle range
  - Filter out doji/small candles
  - Only mark OBs with meaningful body

mitigation_rule: "midpoint" or "full_body"
  - Midpoint: OB invalid once price crosses 50% of zone
  - Full body: OB invalid once price fully inside zone


BEARISH ORDER BLOCK:
--------------------
(Same parameters, mirrored logic)


SWING DETECTION (for structure-based approach):
------------------------------------------------
swing_period: 10-20 candles
  - How many candles left/right to confirm swing

swing_threshold: 1% or 1.5 ATR
  - Minimum movement to qualify as swing


MARKET FILTERS:
---------------
min_atr: Filter out low-volatility periods
  - Example: Only detect OBs when ATR(14) > 1% of price

min_volume: Filter weak OBs
  - Example: OB candle volume > average volume


================================================================================
                    EXAMPLE STRATEGIES USING ORDER BLOCKS
================================================================================

Strategy 1: OB RETEST + CONFIRMATION
-------------------------------------
Entry:
  - Price touches bullish OB (is_in_bullish_ob = TRUE)
  - RSI < 35 (oversold confirmation)
  - 1h trend is bullish (higher TF filter)

Exit:
  - Price reaches bearish OB (supply zone)
  - Take profit: 2% or next liquidity pool
  - Stop loss: Below OB low


Strategy 2: LIQUIDITY GRAB + OB
--------------------------------
Entry:
  - Price sweeps below swing low (liquidity grab)
  - Price reverses back above swing low
  - Price returns to bullish OB
  - Enter on OB touch

Exit:
  - Target: Previous swing high
  - Stop: Below liquidity grab wick


Strategy 3: MULTI-TIMEFRAME OB
-------------------------------
Entry:
  - 4h chart has bullish OB
  - 15m chart shows retest of that OB
  - 15m RSI < 30
  - Enter on 15m

Exit:
  - 4h OB gets mitigated
  - 15m creates bearish OB
